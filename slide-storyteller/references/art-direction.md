# Art Direction

Use this guide when the user wants cute art, clip art, illustration, mascots, stickers, or playful visual storytelling.

## Style Families

Choose one style family per deck:

- soft editorial: warm ink lines, muted color, gentle texture, premium workplace feel
- cheerful sticker: rounded shapes, clean outlines, bright accents, sparse shadows
- cozy notebook: hand-drawn lines, paper texture, margin doodles, personal update feel
- polished product cartoon: simplified people, crisp vector-like shapes, modern SaaS feel
- storybook-lite: charming scenes and characters, restrained enough for adult audiences

## Prompt Recipe

For generated images, specify:

- subject and action
- emotional tone
- visual style family
- palette
- composition and aspect ratio
- transparent background when used as spot art
- no text inside the image unless explicitly needed

Example prompt:

`A small friendly desk calendar character holding a checklist, cheerful sticker illustration, clean rounded shapes, coral and teal accents, transparent background, no text, suitable for a professional weekly update slide.`

## Professional Guardrails

- Use cute art to support the message, not replace evidence.
- Keep characters original and generic unless the user provides a specific licensed or company asset.
- Avoid childish proportions for serious workplace topics.
- Avoid dense scenes behind important text.
- Keep generated art consistent by reusing style family, palette, line weight, and lighting.

## Slide Uses

Good placements:

- one small spot illustration beside a headline
- section divider scene
- recurring status sticker
- empty-state or future-state visual
- simple metaphor: bridge, map, checklist, toolkit, launch pad

Avoid:

- art on every slide when the deck is data-heavy
- decorative art near dense tables
- generated images containing unreadable labels
- fake UI screenshots or fake logos
